- Zeon: Literally conveys boiling, being hot, or burning with enthusiasm

Chapter summary: Paul continues his journey to Jerusalem despite repeated warnings that imprisonment and suffering await him. Upon arriving, he participates in a Jewish purification ritual to address rumors about his teachings, but he is eventually seized by a hostile crowd at the temple and arrested by Roman soldiers, fulfilling the warning given by the prophet Agabus.

"The Lord's will be done." --> Paul is demonstrating once again that remaining faithful to Gods calling can require accepting suffering rather than avoiding it.

1. Did Paul actually teach Jews to abandon the Law of Moses? (Acts 21:20–25)
- No, Paul wasn't teaching Jewish believers that they were forbidden from circumcising their children or observing Jewish customs. However, he did teach that following the Law of Moses was not necessary for salvation.
- Pauls teachings were sometimes misudnerstood because he strongly opposed the idea that circumcision and obedience to the Mosaic Law were necessary for salvation. 

2. Why did Paul participate in the purification ritual? (Acts 21:23–26)
- To address the rumors stated above, Paul participated in the ritual to publicly demonstrate that he wasnt hostile toward Jewish customs and was willing to observe them himself.
- But theres something important to consider: He is willing to respect these customs when doing so didnt comprimise the Gospel.

## Acts 23
Chapter summary: Paul stands before the Sanhedrin, where he creates a dispute between the Pharisees and Sadducees by bringing up his belief in the resurrection of the dead. Later, more than 40 Jews plot to kill him, but his nephew discovers their plan, allowing Roman soldiers to safely transfer Paul to Caesarea, where he awaits trial before Governor Felix.

1. Why did Paul say he was a Pharisee before the Sanhedrin? (Acts 23:1–10)
- Paul really was a Pharisee, or at least someone who had been trained as one and continued to identify with certain Pharisaic beliefs. However, after encountering Jesus, Paul became a follower of Christ. He no longer believed that obedience to the Law of Moses was the means of salvation.

- So why did he identify himself as a Pharisee before the Sanhedrin? --> Acts 23:6 (NIV) explicitly tells us: "Then Paul, knowing that some of them were Sadducees and the others Pharisees, called out in the Sanhedrin..."

This detail is significant because Luke deliberately tells us that Paul knew the council was divided --> So then Paul declares that he is on trial because of his hope in the resurrection of the dead, which immediately creates division because the Pharisees agree with the resurrection while the Sadducees reject it.

2. The Lord encourages Paul (Acts 23:11)
- I really like this verses here, because it shows us that even though Paul knew that suffering awaited him in Jerusalem. In moments like these where you may be lacking some faith or going through a rough patch in your life and feel like the LORD isnt there, he is always there. And this reaffirms it, as Jesus reaffirms and comforts Paul and tells him: "Take courage! Your mission isnt done yet".
- Jesus is so intentional with how he does things, he will make things clear to you, our God is not a God of confusion.
