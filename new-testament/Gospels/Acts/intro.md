## Context of what Acts is
- Acts is known as Lukes continuation or its second volume. The 6 parts of the book of Acts each describe a new phase in the expansion of the Messiah-following movement outward from Jerusalem.

1. Church is established in Jerusalem and becomes Greek-spreaking, allowing it to spread its meessage through the empire.

2. The movement expands into the rest of Palestine

3. Gentiles are included in the gathering of Jesus-followers alongside Jews

4. Messengers are sent west into the Roman provine of Asia

5. Messengers enter Europe

6. Movement reaches the capitable city of Rome and into the highest levels of society; Gods kingdom is thus announced to all nations.

## Intro to Acts
- Acts focuses on what happened afterward the resurrection of Jesus: The birth of the early Church and the spread of Christianity throughout the Roman Empire.

- Luke wrote this book so its a continuation of his Gospel

**2. Who was Acts written to?**
- Theophilus, the same individual addressed in the Gospel of Luke
 
**3. Where was Acts written, and where does it take place?**
There are several important regions:
    Jerusalem: Where the Early Church begins
    Judea & Samaria: Where Christianity first spreads beyond Jerusalem
    Asia Minor (Modern day Turkey): An important region for Pauls missionary journeys
    Greece: Where Christianity spreads into major European cities
    Rome: Capital of the Roman Empire, where the book concludes

**4. Why was Acts written?**
- The primary goal was to document the growth of the early Church and explain how Jesus mission containued after his ascension. This comes with three major themes in the book:

The Holy Spirit: How God empowers and guides Jesus followers
THe spread of the Gospel: How the apostles bring Jesus message beyond Jerusalem to different nations. 
The formation of the Church: How early Christians establish communities, face persecution, and welcome Gentiles into the faith.

**5. What was the historical context?**
Acts beings shortly after Jesus resurrection, around AD 30-33, and follows roughly three decades of early Christian history. At this time:

- The Roman Empire controlled much of the Mediterranean world, including Judea
- Christianity began within Judaism. Jesus earliest followers were Jewish and belived he was the promised Messiah.
- Religious persecution become an important challenge as the apostles encountered opposition from some religious authorities and Roman officials.
- Gentile inclusion became a major question.
