## John 1
Chapter summary: John begins by establishing that Jesus is God, has existed since the beginning, and came into the world as a human being. We then see John the Baptist testify about Jesus, followed by Jesus calling His first disciples.

1. What does "the Word" mean?
- The word refers to Jesus Christ.
- "In the beginning was the Word": Jesus already existed at the beginning of creation. He wasn't created at that point.

- "The Word was with God": Jesus existed in relationship with God the Father. They are distinct persons.

- "The Word was God": Jesus shares the divine nature of God. He is not simply a prophet or a created being.

Why use "the Word"?
- The Greek word John uses is Logos, meaning "Word", "reason", "divine expression". So the idea is that Jesus is Gods ultimate revelation of Himself to humanity.

- Just as our words communicate our thoughts, Jesus reveals Gods nature, character, and will to the world.

John 1:18 reinforces this: Although no one has seen God the Father, Jesus has made the Father known.

2. Wasnt John the Baptist actually Elijah?
- John the Baptist denies being Elijah literally, which is the Old Testament prophet returning in person.
- Jesus identifies John as the fulfillment of the prophecfy that Elijah would come to prepare the way for the Messiah.

- So John fulfills Elijah prophetic role without literally being Elijah reincarned or returned to earth. 

3. John the Baptist identifies Jesus 
- When John sees Jesus he says: "Look, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!".
- This is significant beacuse in the Old Testament, lambs were associated with sacrificies, including sacrifces for sin and the Passover. Calling Jesus the Lamb of God points toward his future sacrificial death on the cross.

## John 2
Zeal = an intense, firey passion, devotion for Gods honor, trut, and commandments.

- Jesus performs His first recorded miracle by turning water into wine at a wedding in Cana. We then shoot forward to when he is traveling to Jerusalem for Passover, where He clears the temple courts and challenges those misusing Gods house.

- An important point is Jesus predicting his resurrection he says: "Destroy this temple, and I will raise it again in three days". Hes referring to His own body and resurrection, not the physical temple. His discples only come to fully understand this after He rises from the dead.

## John 3
- Jesus teaches Nicodemus that entering the Kingdom of God requires being born again through the Holy Spirit. He emphasizes that salvation comes through faith in Him, while John the Baptist reinforces Jesus divine authoirty and explains that his own mission is becoming secondary.

#### Jesus teaching Nicodemus (1-15)
- Jesus is explaining that we cannot earn salvation through our own good deeds. We need spiritual rebirth through the Holy Spirit.

In Verse 8: Jesus compares the Holy Spirit to the wind because: we cannot see the wind itself, but we can see and feel its effects. Similarly we cannot physically see the Holy Spirit, but we can recognize His work in peoples lives. Main point is that the Holy Spirit works in ways beyond our human understanding.

#### Salvation through Jesus (16-21)
- God loves humanity and sent His Son to save the world. Eternal life comes through believing in Jesus, not through good deeds. Those who reject Jesus remain under condemnation. 

#### John the Baptist testifies about Jesus (22-36)
John's disciples notice that Jesus is attracting followers and baptizing, seemingly competing with John's ministry. However, John reminds them that his purpose was never to become the Messiah, but to prepare the way for Him.

- He says that Jesus must become greater; while he becomes less. And then the final verses reinforce that Jesus comes from heaven and possesses authroity above everyone else. While, John is only human.

## John 4
- Reap has 2 definitions

1. Literal (Agricultural meaning)
- Cutting and gather grain crops from a field using a tool.

2. Figurative
- Gaining, winning, or earning something as a direct result of previous actions or efforts.

Chapter summary: Jesus speaks with a Samaritan woman about the living water that brings eternal life, leading many Samaritans to believe in Him. He then teaches His disciples about spreading God's message and later heals an official's son in Galilee.

- Jesus speaking with a Samaritan is surprising because he is crossing cultural and social boundaries.

- Then Jesus speaks about how the water that he gives will lead people to not be thirsty ever again. Because it will satisfy us Spiritually, he gives us lasting hope and purpose, but this doesnt mean that we wont want other things as well.

- The Samaritan woman asks about the correct location for worship because Samaritans worshiped on Mount Gerizim, while Jews worshiped in Jerusalem. Jesus explains that true worship will no longer depend on a particular geographical location.

- The point about the fields being ripe for harvest: The harvest represents people who are ready to receive God's message and believe in Jesus. Jesus is telling His disciples that they shouldn't wait. The opportunity to bring people to God is already in  front of them.

#### The sower and the reaper
- This is referring to different roles in spreading Gods message.
The sower is someone who plants the seeds of faith by sharing Gods message
The reaper is someone who sees the results when people respond to that message.

## John 5
Chapter Summary: Jesus heals a disabled man on the Sabbath, leading to conflict with the Jewish religious leaders. Jesus then explains His divine authority, His relationship with the Father, and His power to grant eternal life and judge humanity

- Jesus heals at the pool and He doesn't seek public recognition for the miracle and quietly slips away into the crowd.

#### The authority of the Son
- Jesus is establishing that he possesses divine authority given to Him by the Father.

- The Son has authority to judge humanity, God the Father doesnt judge. And then another big point is that Faith brings eternal life: Those who believe in Jesus and the Father who sent Him have crossed from spiritual death into eternal life. 

Jesus says that a time is coming when everyone in their graves will hear His voice and come out, with different outcomes for those who have done good and those who have done evil.
This is referring to the future resurrection and final judgment, not specifically to Jesus' crucifixion.

#### Testimonies about Jesus
- Jesus challenges the religiosu leaders because they claim to believe in God and the Scriptures, yet reject the very person those Scriptures point toward. He identifies several witnesses supporting his identity.

- John the Baptist, who testified about Jesus.
- Jesus' miracles, which demonstrate that the Father sent Him.
- God the Father, who testifies concerning His Son.
- The Scriptures and Moses, whose writings point toward Jesus.

The irony is that they diligently study Scripture in search of eternal life but reject Jesus, whom Scripture points toward as the source of that life.

## John 6
- Chapter Summary: Jesus feeds the 5,000 and walks on water before teaching that He is the Bread of Life, the source of eternal life. His teachings are difficult for many to accept, causing several disciples to abandon Him.

#### Jesus is the Bread of Life (25-40)
- When Jesus says not to work for food that spoils, He's contrasting temporary physical needs with eternal spiritual needs.
- Physical food: Sustains our bodies temporarily, but we'll eventually become hungry again.
- Spiritual food (Jesus): Gives us eternal life through faith in Him.
- Jesus isn't saying we should neglect physical needs, but that our relationship with God should take priority over temporary worldly concerns.

#### Eating Jesus flesh and drinking His blood (41-59)
- Jesus is the source of spiritual life, and we must depend on Him to receive eternal life.

- Jesus also repeatedly says this throughout the chapter: "I will raise them up at the last day" --> Jesus is promising that those who believe in Him will receive eternal life and be raised at the final resurrection.

## John 7
- Chapter Summary: Jesus attends the Festival of Tabernacles in Jerusalem, where He teaches publicly and faces increasing opposition from religious leaders. People are divided over whether He is the Messiah, while Jesus continues emphasizing His divine authority and offering eternal life

**"My time has not yet come"** --> Jesus repeatedly emphasizes that His mission unfolds according to the Fathers timing, not human expectations. Nothing will happen that will deviate from Gods plan. 

#### The division over Jesus identity
- People were confused because of their expectations about the Messiah. Some believed Jesus couldn't be the Messiah because He came from Galilee, whereas the Scriptures indicated that the Messiah would come from Bethlehem and David's family. The irony is that Jesus actually was born in Bethlehem, as we've read in Matthew and Luke.

- We once again see this metaphor of thirst representing humanitys spiritual need. Jesus will satisy our deepest spirirtual needs fully.

## John 8
Chapter summary: Jesus continues teaching in the temple courts, boldly declaring that He comes from the Father and speaks the truth. He challenges the religious leaders for rejecting Him and explains that those who follow Him will receive eternal life.

There are a couple of things worth highlighting:
- Jesus is the Light of the World: Those who follow Him will no longer walk in spiritual darkness.
- The truth will set you free: Jesus explains that everyone who sins is a slave to sin.

- We also have an extra passage at the beginning of John 8 where a woman is caught in adultery. The people in the temple want to stone her as it is written in "Moses Law". But Jesus says okay, whoever is free of sin can do this, and everyone slowly walks away. I think this speaks volumes into how we cant believe we are better than someone or have the right to condem or judge them. It also shows Jesus mercy and call to repentance which is beautiful.

## John 9
Chapter summary: Jesus heals a man who was blind from birth, but the Pharisees refuse to accept that Jesus comes from God. The chapter contrasts the growing faith of the healed man with the spiritual blindness of the religious leaders.

- The point that Mircales dont guarantee faith has really shown up in this chapter and it feels like we are connecting the dots from this idea that we have seen in previous books:
- Miracles don't guarantee faith: In Luke 16:31, where Abraham says that if people refuse to listen to Moses and the prophets, they won't be convinced even if someone rises from the dead. John 9 illustrates a similar principle.

- The blind man exposes the Pharisees inconsistency in their argument. They claim that Jesus is a sinner, yet Jesus has performed extraordinary healing that points toward Gods power. And that if what the Pharisees are saying is really true that he is Sinner and is away from God, he could never do these things.

#### Spiritual blindness
Jesus contrasts two kinds of blindness:
- Physical blindness: The man couldn't see, but Jesus restored his sight.
- Spiritual blindness: The Pharisees could physically see, but they refused to recognize the truth about Jesus.

## John 10
Chapter summary: Jesus describes Himself as the Good Shepherd who protects, guides, and sacrifices His life for His sheep. He emphasizes His relationship with His followers and the Father,

- Jesus is the gate: He is the way to salvation. Those who enter through Him receive eternal life. You cannot lose your salvation.
- One flock, one shepard: This speaks about unity in Christianity, there are many different denominations and we have these arguments about who is right and whatever, but at the end of the day we need to remember that we are one body of christ. 


## John 11
- Mary, Martha, and Lazarus were all siblings. They were good friends of Jesus. This is not Mary Magdalene instead it is Mary of Bethany.

Chapter summary: Jesus deliberately waits before traveling to Lazarus, who dies from his illness. Jesus then raises Lazarus from the dead, demonstrating His power over death and leading many people to believe in Him. The miracle also intensifies the religious leaders' plans to kill Jesus.

#### Why did Jesus allow Lazarus to die instead of healing him?
- To glorify God: Raising someone from the dead demonstrates divine authority. It also shows that Jesus has authority over life and death itself.
- To strengthen peoples faith.

- Lazarus resurrection serves as a physical demonstration of the eternal life Jesus promises to believers. Physical death doesnt have the final word. This connects to when Jesus tells Martha: "I am the resurrection and the life. The one who believes in me will live, even though they die"

#### Why did Jesus weep
- Yes this shows his genuine humanity and emotions. But more deeply this Jesus expressing sorrow and anger toward the destructuve reality of death.

Then at the end in the plot to kill Jesus:
- Caiaphas, the high priest, declares that it would be better for one man to die than for the entire nation to perish.
- John explains that Caiaphas unknowingly prophesies something greater: Jesus would die not only for the Jewish nation, but also to bring together God's scattered children.
