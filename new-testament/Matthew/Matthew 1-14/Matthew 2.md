- After Jesus was born in Bethlehem some Magi (wise men) from the east came to Jerusalem to ask where has the king of the Jews been born because they saw his star.

- They come to Jerusalem to ask King Herod which was the king of the time. Its also noted that the Messiah being born in Bethlehem answers the prophecy.

- Herod calls the Magi to find out where Jesus had been born, plotting to secretly kill him, after the Magi find Jesus, they presented him with gifts and worshipped him, and then returns to their country by another route after having been warned by a dream to not go back through Jerusalem.

- Then Joseph was told by an angel to escape to Egypt because Herod was actually planning to kill Jesus. When Herod realizes that he had been outwitted he gave order to kill all the boys in Bethlehem and its vicinity who were 2 years old and under.

- This answers another prophecy saying that there would be a weeping and great mourning for dead children.

- After Herod dies, Joseph in Egypt is told to return to Israel by an angel, but since Herod son (Archealus) was regining in Judea now, he was worned to go to the district of Galilee instead.
- And he went and lived in a town called Nazareth and therefore the prophecy that the messiah would be called a Nazarene.

### Important point:
- The Magi are Gentiles, meaning they are not Jews, yet they recognize that something extraordinary has happened and come to worship Jesus. Meanwhile, Herod—the king ruling over the Jewish people—tries to kill Him. That contrast is significant and becomes a theme throughout Matthew: Jesus is Israel’s Messiah, but His significance extends far beyond Israel.

- Jesus going into and returning from Egypt is important. Matthew quotes Hosea: “Out of Egypt I called my son.” Originally, Hosea was talking about Israel being brought out of Egypt during the Exodus. Matthew deliberately applies that language to Jesus. In other words, Jesus’ life begins to resemble the story of Israel itself.
