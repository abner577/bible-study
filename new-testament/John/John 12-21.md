## John 12
Chapter summary: Jesus is anointed at Bethany, enters Jerusalem as King, and predicts His coming death. He emphasizes that following Him requires putting God above our earthly lives and warns that those who reject Him are also rejecting the Father.

The point about Losing your life to save it --> Jesus is teaching that we must prioritize following Him over wordly attachments, comfort, and even our physical survival when necessary, its about surrending our lives to God.

- Jesus urges people to believe in Him while He is still physically among them. The light represents His truth and the salvation He offers.

#### Isaiahs prophecy (38-40)
- John quotes two passages from Isaiah to explain why so many people still refuse to believe in Jesus despite witnessing His miracles.

- John is explaining that peoples rejection of Gods message was already described in Scripture. Even though Jesus performed miraculous signs, many refused to believe. And then verse 40 --> It presents the idea that presistent rejection of Gods truth leads to spiritual blindness and hardened hearts.

John explains that even some religious leaders believed in Jesus but wouldn't publicly acknowledge Him because they feared being expelled from the synagogue.
Why?
"For they loved human praise more than praise from God." (John 12:43)

The lesson: Fear of other people's opinions can prevent us from openly following God, even when we recognize the truth.

## John 13
Chapter summary: Jesus washes His disciples' feet to teach them humility and selfless service. He then predicts Judas' betrayal and Peter's denial before giving His disciples a new commandment: to love one another as He has loved them.

1. Why does Jesus wash His disciples feet?
- Jesus is demonstrating humility but there is a deeper significance. In that time, people walked in sandals on dusty roads, so washing someone's feet was typically a task performed by servants. Jesus, the Son of God, their Lord and Teacher, willingly takes the position of a servant and washes His disciples' feet.

Jesus is teaching them important lessons:
- Humility and service --> If Jesus is willing to serve others in such a humble way. No one is too important to serve another person
- Spiritual cleansing --> It also points to our need of being cleansed by Jesus

2. Jesus predicts Judas betrayal
- This is a unique detail John includes. Jesus actually identifies Judas by handing him the dipped bread. 

3. New commandment: Love one another
- Jesus isnt simply saying to love others. Hes tellign them to love with the same humility, sacrifice and forgiveness that He has demonstrated. And a reminder that our behavior should serve as a living testimony of the faith we profess.

## John 14
Chapter summary: Jesus comforts His disciples as He prepares for His death, promising them eternal life with the Father. He emphasizes that He is the only way to salvation and promises to send the Holy Spirit to guide, teach, and strengthen His followers.

1. Jesus and the Father are one (pointing to the Holy Trinity)
- Jesus explains that He and the Father are distinct persons but share the same divine nature. When Jesus says that seeing Him is seeing the Father, He's explaining that He perfectly reveals who the Father is. The Trinity is three distinct persons sharing one divine being.

2. The famous Verse: John 14:6 (I am the way and the truth and the life)
- Jesus is explicitly declaring that He is the only way to the Father and eternal salvation.

- Jesus also promises the Holy Spirit, that when He physically departs, His followers wont be abandoned.

- Then in verse 27 Jesus says: "My peace I give you" --> Jesus isnt promising that His followers will never experience hardship, fear, or suffering --> Rather he offers a deeper peace rooted in trusting God, even in tough times.

## John 15
Chapter summary: Jesus describes Himself as the true vine and His followers as the branches, emphasizing that spiritual life and growth are only possible through Him. He commands His disciples to love one another and warns them that the world may reject them just as it rejected Him.

1. The vine and the branches
- Jesus is the vine: He is the source of spiritual life, juts as branches depend on a vine for nourishment, we depend on Jesus.
- We are the branches: We must remain connected to Jesus through genuine faith to bear spiritual fruit.
- Bearing fruit: This doesn't refer exclusively to making disciples. It also includes demonstrating love, obedience, faithfulness, and Christlike character.
- Pruning: God works in the lives of fruitful believers to help them grow spiritually. Pruning can involve correction, discipline, and removing things that hinder spiritual growth.
- Branches thrown into the fire: This imagery is commonly understood as warning of divine judgment for those who do not remain in Christ.

2. Love one another
- Jesus continues reinforcing the commandment we encountered in John 13 --> Greater love has no one than this: to lay down ones life for ones friends --> This foreshadows His crucifixion and illustrates the sacrificial love He expects His followers to demonstrate. Because right after this he called them his friends.

3. The world hating the discciples
- Being "not of the world" doesn't mean rejecting everything earthly or that every non-Christian will hate you. In this context, the world refers especially to humanity organized in opposition to God and His teachings.

- Jesus explains that His followers shouldnt conform to this world in contradicting to Gods commandments and will. Also that the world will reject you, because you are different from them, you are not of this world and you dont partake in the things that the people of this world partake in. But no fear, beacuse if they are to accept you, then you are of them and not with God. 

## John 16
Chapter summary: Jesus prepares His disciples for His departure, explaining that the Holy Spirit will come to guide them into truth. He warns them of the suffering ahead but promises that their grief will turn into joy because He has overcome the world.

This chapter provides important foundations for the Christian doctrine of the Holy Trinity. Jesus is explaining that the Holy Spirit will:
- Convict the world of sin, righteousness, and judgement, guide believers into truth, and glorify Jesus.

1. Who is the "prince of this world"
- This refers to Satan, he is described this way because of his influence over the sinful world and its opposition to God.

2. Why does Jesus speak figuratively before speaking clearly?
- Jesus explains that He's been speaking through figures of speech, but a time is coming when He will speak plainly about the Father.
- The main idea is that the disciples' understanding of Jesus and His mission is still incomplete.
- After His death and resurrection, and through the Holy Spirit's guidance, they will understand much more clearly what He has been teaching them.

## John 17
- Sanctify = To make holy, purify, or set apart for a sacred purpose

Chapter summary: Before His arrest and crucifixion, Jesus prays for Himself, His disciples, and all future believers. He asks the Father to glorify Him, protect His followers, and unite all believers in faith and love.\

1. Jesus prays to be glorified
- Jesus is saying that the time has come for Him to filfill His purpose through His death, resurrection, and return to the Father.
- Glorify your Son: Jesus asks the Father to reveal His divine glory through the completion of His mission.
- That your Son may glorify you: Through His sacrifice and resurrection, Jesus will reveal God's love, power, and plan for salvation.

- Jesus knows that He will son leave His disciples, so He asks the Father to protect them as they continue His mission.

2. Jesus prays for all believers
- "That all of them may be one, Father, just as you are in me and I am in you."
- Jesus is asking that His followers be united in faith, love, and their relationship with God. Jesus is emphasizing a deeper spiritual unity root between christians beacuse they all share that relationship wit Jesus and the Father.
