## Introduction to John
- Although the Gospel of John tells the story of the same Jesus, it approaches the story differently. John focuses much more on who Jesus is, His relationship with God the Father, and why believing in Him matters for eternal life.

1. Who was John?
- The Gospel is attributed to John the Apostle, one of Jesus twelve disciples. He was originally a fisherman before Jesus called him to become a disciple.
- Along with Peter and James, John belonged to Jesus closest circle of disciples.

- The supposed author is John althought it isnt 100% known.

2. Who was John writing to?
- It doesnt seem like he was specifically addressing one person, instead the Gospel is intended for a broader audience, literally everyone: Jewish, Gentile, Christians, potential delievers.

3. What was the historical context

Let's put John's Gospel into the timeline we've been following.
1. AD 30–33 — Jesus' crucifixion and resurrection
   Jesus completes His earthly ministry. His disciples begin spreading His teachings.
2. AD 30s–60s — Christianity spreads
   The apostles preach throughout the Roman Empire. Both Jews and Gentiles become followers of Jesus.
3. AD 70 — Destruction of Jerusalem's Temple
   The Romans destroy the Second Temple in Jerusalem, profoundly affecting Jewish religious life and the environment in which Christianity develops.
4. AD 90–100 — Commonly proposed period for John's Gospel
   Several decades after Jesus' resurrection, the Gospel is likely written in its final form as Christian communities continue developing their understanding of Jesus.

- By the time John was likely written, Christianity had spread well beyond Jerusalem. There were also growing disagreements over Jesus' identity, particularly between some followers of Jesus and other Jewish communities.
- This context helps explain why John spends so much time emphasizing Jesus' divine identity and His relationship with the Father.

4. What was the purpose of Johns Gospel?
John wants his readers to understand three things:

**1. Jesus is the Messiah:** The promise Savior spoken about throughout the Old Testament
**2. Jesus is the Son of God: His identity goes beyond being a prophet or miracle worker. He is divine.**
**3. Believing in Jesus brings eternal life: Through faith in Him, people can receive salvation.**
