## John 1
Chapter summary: John begins by establishing that Jesus is God, has existed since the beginning, and came into the world as a human being. We then see John the Baptist testify about Jesus, followed by Jesus calling His first disciples.

1. What does "the Word" mean?
- The word refers to Jesus Christ.
- "In the beginning was the Word": Jesus already existed at the beginning of creation. He wasn't created at that point.

- "The Word was with God": Jesus existed in relationship with God the Father. They are distinct persons.

- "The Word was God": Jesus shares the divine nature of God. He is not simply a prophet or a created being.

Why use "the Word"?
- The Greek word John uses is Logos, meaning "Word", "reason", "divine expression". So the idea is that Jesus is Gods ultimate revelation of Himself to humanity.

- Just as our words communicate our thoughts, Jesus reveals Gods nature, character, and will to the world.

John 1:18 reinforces this: Although no one has seen God the Father, Jesus has made the Father known.

2. Wasnt John the Baptist actually Elijah?
- John the Baptist denies being Elijah literally, which is the Old Testament prophet returning in person.
- Jesus identifies John as the fulfillment of the prophecfy that Elijah would come to prepare the way for the Messiah.

- So John fulfills Elijah prophetic role without literally being Elijah reincarned or returned to earth. 

3. John the Baptist identifies Jesus 
- When John sees Jesus he says: "Look, the Lamb of God, who takes away the sin of the world!".
- This is significant beacuse in the Old Testament, lambs were associated with sacrificies, including sacrifces for sin and the Passover. Calling Jesus the Lamb of God points toward his future sacrificial death on the cross.

## John 2
Zeal = an intense, firey passion, devotion for Gods honor, trut, and commandments.

- Jesus performs His first recorded miracle by turning water into wine at a wedding in Cana. We then shoot forward to when he is traveling to Jerusalem for Passover, where He clears the temple courts and challenges those misusing Gods house.

- An important point is Jesus predicting his resurrection he says: "Destroy this temple, and I will raise it again in three days". Hes referring to His own body and resurrection, not the physical temple. His discples only come to fully understand this after He rises from the dead.

## John 3
- Jesus teaches Nicodemus that entering the Kingdom of God requires being born again through the Holy Spirit. He emphasizes that salvation comes through faith in Him, while John the Baptist reinforces Jesus divine authoirty and explains that his own mission is becoming secondary.

#### Jesus teaching Nicodemus (1-15)
- Jesus is explaining that we cannot earn salvation through our own good deeds. We need spiritual rebirth through the Holy Spirit.

In Verse 8: Jesus compares the Holy Spirit to the win because: we cannot see the wind itself, but we can see and feel its effects. Similarly we cannot physically see the Holy Spirit, but we can recognize His work in peoples lives. Main point is that the Holy Spirit works in ways beyond our human understanding.

#### Salvation through Jesus (16-21)
- God loves humanity and sent His Son to save the world. Eternal life comes through believing in Jesus, not through good deeds. Those who reject Jesus remain under condemnation. 

#### John the Baptist testifies about Jesus (22-36)
John's disciples notice that Jesus is attracting followers and baptizing, seemingly competing with John's ministry. However, John reminds them that his purpose was never to become the Messiah, but to prepare the way for Him.

- He says that Jesus must become greater; while he becomes less. And then the final verses reinforce that Jesus comes from heaven and possesses authroity above everyone else. While, John is only human.
