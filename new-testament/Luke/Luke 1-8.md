## Luke 1
- This chapter tells the beginnings of John the Baptist and Jesus side by side. 

- The angel Gabriel goes to Zechariah first and tells him that Elizabeth a woman that is seemingly unable to have children, will have John, Zechariah doubts because they are old and Elizabeth has been unable to have children, so he is temporarily unable to speak because of his lack of faith. Gabriel later appears separately to Mary and announces Jesus’ birth and she trust in the word wholy.

John's birth announced → miraculous birth to an elderly, previously childless couple.
Jesus' birth announced → an even greater miracle: Mary will conceive while still a virgin through the Holy Spirit.
Mary visits Elizabeth → John, still in Elizabeth's womb, reacts to Jesus' presence, already pointing toward John's future role of preparing the way for Jesus.
Mary's song (the Magnificat) praises God for keeping His promises, showing mercy, lifting up the humble, and fulfilling His covenant promises to Israel.
John is born, Zechariah confirms his name, regains his speech, and then gives his own prophetic song.
Zechariah's song celebrates God's coming salvation and specifically describes John's purpose: he will "go on before the Lord to prepare the way for him."

So the biggest takeaway from Luke 1 is: God's long-promised plan is finally beginning to unfold. John is being prepared as the messenger who will announce and prepare the way, while Jesus is the promised Savior and King toward whom everything is pointing.

## Luke 5
- We get the story of how Jesus calls his first disciples: Simon Peter, James & John sons of Zebeedee

- Jesus heals a man with leprosy and a paralyzed man.

- Jesus eats with sinners and says this: "I have not come to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance."

- Then while eating with sinners he is questioned about fasting:

First, Jesus uses the picture of a bridegroom at a wedding. His point is: while the bridegroom is present, the guests are celebrating, so it would be strange for them to mourn and fast. Jesus is the bridegroom. While He is physically with His disciples, this is a time of joy.

- His parables about new patch on an old garment and new wine in old wineskins means --> What He is bringing cannot simply be squeezed into the old religious framework and practices as if nothing has changed. 

- His arrival marks something new in Gods plan, not just a small adjustment to the old system.
