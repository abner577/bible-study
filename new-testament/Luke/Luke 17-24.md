## Luke 17
- In the beginning of this chapter, Jesus teaches about sin, forgiveness, faith, and humility. The main point is the servant example and being obedient. We arent obedient to put God in our debt or to think that we deserve to be in the kingdom of God or we are owed something. We are worthless servants that are ultimately sinners without Gods grace.

- So we obey God because he is Lord and we are His servants, that is our duty.

#### The coming of the kingdom of God
- It has two parts, at the beginniong the Pharisees ask when the kingdom of God will come and Jesus tells them that "the kingdom of God is in your midst" --> In other words, Gods kingdom has already begun to arrive through Jesus Himself. 

- Then Jesus is describing his second coming as life will seem completely normal right up until judgment suddently arrived. Thats why Jesus says His coming will be like "lighting flashing across the sky" it will not be some hidden event that people need to hunt down. When the Son of Man is revealed, it will be unmistakable.

## Luke 18
- For the persistent widow parable, the main point is exactly that believers should keep praying and not lose heart. His justice will ultimately come in His timing, God is listening.

- Then the parable of the Pharisees and the tax collector its another strong lesson on humility.
**"For all those who exalt themselves will be humbled, and those who humble themselves will be exalted"**

- Then through the rich ruler is once again reiterating the idea that if you are attached and have pride toward your earthly posessions and make that the center of your life it will be very hard for you to enter the kingdom of God beacuse its so hard to put that second when its such a big part of your identity.

## Luke 19
**The parable of the ten minas**
- The basic setup is that a nobleman goes away to receive a kingdom, gives money to his servants, and tells them to put it to work until he returns. The nobleman represents Jesus. His going away points to Jesus leaving for a time, and his return represents the time when He returns and His servants are held accountable.

- The servants represent people who have been entrusted with something by God. The minas can therefore represent what God has given us to use faithfully — our knowledge of Him, opportunities, abilities, resources, responsibilities.

- The main point is that his followers are supposed to live faithfully and productively for His kingdom rather than doing nothing with what He entrusted to them.

**Verses 42-44 Why is Jesus crying over Jerusalem?**
- This happens as Jesus approaches Jerusalem during the triumphal entry and he essentialy says: "If only you understood what would bring you peace but you dont see it".

- Then he predicts that enemies will surrodn Jerusalem, tear it down and destroy its people --> Because they didnt recognize the time of Gods coming to you. This prophecy is understood as pointing toward the Roman destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70.

## Luke 20
Not really any new content it just intensifies the conflict between Jesus and the religious leaders.

- The chapter begins with the chief priests and teachers of the law questioning Jesus’ authority. They are not really asking because they want to learn; they are trying to force Him into an answer they can use against Him.

- Then comes the parable of the tenants, and your interpretation is correct. The vineyard represents what God has entrusted to His people, the servants represent the prophets God repeatedly sent, and the owner's son represents Jesus. The tenants reject and mistreat the servants and finally kill the son.

Then you get the familiar attempts to trap Jesus like with the Sadducees question about resurrection using the hypothetical woman who had 7 husbands, and should we pay taxes to Caesar question.
