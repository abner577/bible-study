## Luke 17
- In the beginning of this chapter, Jesus teaches about sin, forgiveness, faith, and humility. The main point is the servant example and being obedient. We arent obedient to put God in our debt or to think that we deserve to be in the kingdom of God or we are owed something. We are worthless servants that are ultimately sinners without Gods grace.

- So we obey God because he is Lord and we are His servants, that is our duty.

#### The coming of the kingdom of God
- It has two parts, at the beginniong the Pharisees ask when the kingdom of God will come and Jesus tells them that "the kingdom of God is in your midst" --> In other words, Gods kingdom has already begun to arrive through Jesus Himself. 

- Then Jesus is describing his second coming as life will seem completely normal right up until judgment suddently arrived. Thats why Jesus says His coming will be like "lighting flashing across the sky" it will not be some hidden event that people need to hunt down. When the Son of Man is revealed, it will be unmistakable.

## Luke 18

- Then the parable of the Pharisees and the tax collector its another strong lesson on humility.
**"For all those who exalt themselves will be humbled, and those who humble themselves will be exalted"**

## Luke 19
**The parable of the ten minas**
- The basic setup is that a nobleman goes away to receive a kingdom, gives money to his servants, and tells them to put it to work until he returns. The nobleman represents Jesus. His going away points to Jesus leaving for a time, and his return represents the time when He returns and His servants are held accountable.

- The servants represent people who have been entrusted with something by God. The minas can therefore represent what God has given us to use faithfully — our knowledge of Him, opportunities, abilities, resources, responsibilities.

- The main point is that his followers are supposed to live faithfully and productively for His kingdom rather than doing nothing with what He entrusted to them.

**Verses 42-44 Why is Jesus crying over Jerusalem?**
- This happens as Jesus approaches Jerusalem during the triumphal entry and he essentialy says: "If only you understood what would bring you peace but you dont see it".

- Then he predicts that enemies will surround Jerusalem, tear it down and destroy its people --> Because they didnt recognize the time of Gods coming to you. This prophecy is understood as pointing toward the Roman destruction of Jerusalem in AD 70.

## Luke 20
Not really any new content it just intensifies the conflict between Jesus and the religious leaders.

- The chapter begins with the chief priests and teachers of the law questioning Jesus’ authority. They are not really asking because they want to learn; they are trying to force Him into an answer they can use against Him.

- Then comes the parable of the tenants. The vineyard represents what God has entrusted to His people, the servants represent the prophets God repeatedly sent, and the owner's son represents Jesus. The tenants reject and mistreat the servants and finally kill the son.

Then you get the familiar attempts to trap Jesus like with the Sadducees question about resurrection using the hypothetical woman who had 7 husbands, and should we pay taxes to Caesar question.

## Luke 21
This chapter is very similar to Matthew 24 and Mark 13. The chapter talks about two main things together:

1. Warning about the coming destructon of Jerusalem and the temple (AD 70 Roman destruction)
2. End time and the return of the Son of Man.

- And the main takeaway as it has been throughout chapters like this is to stay spiritually alert and faithful rather than obessing over the exact date.

## Luke 22
This covers alot of things that we have already seen such as the Last Supper, Mount of Olives, Jesus betrayal, Peter disowning Jesus 3 times before Rooster crows.

- But Luke 22 adds an extra detail: “Simon, Simon, Satan has asked to sift all of you as wheat. But I have prayed for you, Simon, that your faith may not fail.”

- Jesus knows Peter is about to fail, but He also knows that Peters failure will not be the end of his story. This shows that God puts us through tough things and allows us to fail to build us up back stronger.

**Jesus praying on the Mount of Olives** --> Jesus genuinely doesnt desire the great suffering he knows he is going to endure so he Prays: "“Father, if you are willing, take this cup from me.”" But its important that he says: “Yet not my will, but yours be done.”

- So while Jesus is experiencing very real anguish and great, yet He remains completely obedient to the Father.

**Luke 23:28-31**
- Jesus turns to the women who are mourning for Him and basically says: Don’t only weep for me. Weep for yourselves and for what is coming upon Jerusalem.

He then describes a future time of such terrible suffering that people will say things that would normally sound shocking — like calling childlessness a blessing — because the coming disaster will be so severe. 

Then the idea of the green and dry tree:
- The idea is basically: If an innocent Jesus can be treated this way now, while God’s Messiah is among them, imagine how severe things will become when judgment falls on a spiritually hardened people.


Then we get more details in Jesus crucifixion:
- We know that there are two criminals next to Jesus that are also getting crucified next to him. One of the criminals mocks Jesus, and the other one rebukes him and Jesus says that he will be saved.

- And this is really important beacuse it drives home the point that the criminal has nothing left to offer Jesus. He cannot go perform good works, fix his past, or prove himself. 

## Luke 24
Covers Jesus ressurection and briefly his ascension

- Luke gives us extra details especially the road to Emmaus What makes that story important is that they are confused and discouraged because Jesus has died. They had hoped He was the one who would redeem Israel, and now they do not understand how His death fits with that hope.
- Jesus walks with them, but they do not recognize Him at first. He then explains how the Law and the Prophets pointed toward the Messiah suffering and then entering His glory. In other words, He helps them understand:
- The crucifixion was not a failure of God’s plan. It was part of God’s plan.
- Then, when He breaks bread with them, they recognize Him, and He disappears.

And then a unique sec tion where he makes it a point that he is phyiscally revealing himself to his discples.
