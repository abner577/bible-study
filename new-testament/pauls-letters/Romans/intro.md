## Context

- Were moving into a new section of the New Testament: Paul`s letters, also known as the Pauline Epistles.

- Romans focuses on explaining what Jesus` life, death, and resurrection mean for humanity.

## 1. Who wrote Romans?
- The apostle Paul. Paul was originally known as Saul, a Pharisee who persecuted Christians. In Acts 9, Jesus appeared to him on the road to Damascus, completely changing his life. Paul became one of the most important figures in spreading Christianity, particularly among the Gentiles (non-Jews).

- Romans was written before Paul arrived in Rome as a prisoner.

## 2. Who was Romans written to?
- The Christians living in Rome, the capital of the Roman Empire. The Christian community in Rome consisted of both:
- **Jewish Christians:** Jews who believed Jesus was the promised Messiah
- **Gentile Christians:** Non-Jews who had converted to Christianity

- We touch on this distinction between jewish and gentile christians a lot in this book: Paul explains that both Jews and Gentiles need salvation, and both can receive it through faith in Jesus Christ. Neither group has a privileged path to salvation simply because of its ancestry.

## 3. When and where was Romans written?
- Most Likely Corinth, a Greek city where Paul spent time during his missionary journeys.

Heres how Romans fits into the timeline of Acts:
1. Acts 18–20 — Paul's missionary journeys
- Paul travels throughout Greece and Macedonia, strengthening churches and spreading Christianity.

2. Around Acts 20:2–3 — Paul writes Romans
- During his roughly three-month stay in Greece, probably in Corinth, Paul writes to the Christians in Rome.

3. Acts 21–26 — Paul's arrest and imprisonment
- Paul returns to Jerusalem, gets arrested, and eventually appeals to Caesar.

4. Acts 27–28 — Paul finally arrives in Rome
- Paul reaches Rome as a prisoner, several years after writing his letter.

- When Paul wrote Romans, he hadnt visited the Roman Christian community yet, but he was hoping to do so.

## 4. Why did Paul write Romans?
    1. To explain the message of salvation
    - Paul wanted the Christians in Rome to understand the foundation of the Christian faith. He addreses questions such as:
    - Why do humans need salvation? Why can't following the Law of Moses alone save someone? How does faith in Jesus make someone righteous before God? What role do grace, sin, and forgiveness play in salvation?

    2. To encourage unity between Jewish and Gentile Christians
    - Remember that the Jewish people had centuries of history with God through Abraham, Moses, the prophets, and the Law. Whereas the Gentiles didnt share this same history, so naturally this raised questions about how these 2 groups fit into Gods plan.

    3. To prepare for his future visit to Rome
    - Paul wanted to visit the Roman Christians, encourage them, and eventually continue his missionary work into Spain. He hoped the Roman Christians would help support that mission. Romans therefore also served as an introduction to Paul and his teachings.


## 5. What are the main themes of Romans
Chapters	Main focus
1–3	Humanity's sin and why everyone needs salvation
4–5	Being made righteous through faith, using Abraham as an example
6–8	Living a new life through Christ and the Holy Spirit
9–11	Israel, the Jewish people, and God's promises
12–15	How Christians should live and treat one another
16	Personal greetings and Paul's closing message

- One of the most important ideas throughout the entire letter is **justification by faith**. In simple terms justification means being declared righteous (being put right with) God. Paul explains that we cannot earn this standing before God simply by following religious laws or performing good deeds. Instead, it comes through Gods grace and is received through faith in Jesus Christ.
