## Romans 5 
trespass = violation of moral law or trust that breaches a relationship with God or another person.

Chapter summary: Paul explains that through faith in Jesus Christ, we are justified, reconciled with God, and given peace with Him and the hope of eternal life. He then contrasts Adam, whose disobedience brought sin and death into the world, with Jesus Christ, whose obedience and sacrifice bring grace, righteousness, and eternal life to those who receive God's gift.

1. Peace with God
- Here Paul is talking about us having peace with God, this is reffering to our restored relationship with God. We are no longer in a state of hostility toward God because of our sin. We have been reconciled to Him through Christ.

- Christ died for us while we were still sinners, none of us deserved Christ sacrifice on the basis of our righteousness.

2. Romans 5:12-14 What does it mean that sin isnt charged where there is no law
- Romans 2:12-16 actually touches on this --> Where Paul explains that Gentiles who dont posses the written Law still have a conscience that bears witness to moral requirements. 

3. Adams trespass versus Christ gift
- Here Paul is contrasting the consequences of Adam`s disobedience with the consequences of Christ obedience
- If one mans disobedience could have such enormouse conseuqences, how much greater must the impact of Jesus sacrifice be. Paul uses the expression: "how much more" --> Paul presents Gods grace through Christ as surpassing the destructive consequences of Adam`s sin

4. Why did the Law cause trespasses to increase?
"The law was brought in so that the trespass might increase. But where sin increased, grace increased all the more." (Romans 5:20, NIV)

- Why would God introduce His Law if doing so would cause sin to increase? -->
    1. First, the Law makes sin more recognizable and exposes its seriousness. 
    - Once we have clear commandments about what we should and should not to --> Thier wrongdoing becomes an explicit violation of a known rule --> This connects to this verse because it makes us more conscious of sin

    2. The Law can actually provoke further transgression in sinful humnaity
    - This idea that sinful human desires rebel against Gods commandments. Pauls point is that the problem lies in humanitys sinful nature. 

"But where sin increased, grace increased all the more" --> Even though sin increased, Gods grace through Jesus Christ was greater. The point is that no matter how extensive humanitys sin has become, Gods saving grace through Christ is more than sufficient to overcome it.

## Romans 6
Chapter summary: Paul explains that being saved by God's grace does not give us permission to continue living in sin. Through our union with Jesus Christ, we have died to our old sinful way of life and have been raised to live a new life devoted to God. He contrasts slavery to sin, which leads to death, with obedience to God, which leads to holiness and eternal life.

1. Romans 6:2-7 Baptized into Christs death
What does Paul mean when he says we're baptized into Christ's death and buried with Him through baptism?
- Paul is describing how becoming united with Jesus Christ means participating in the significance of His death and resurrection. He is trying to draw a connection to Christ life and our lives -->
Jesus died, was buried, and rose again to a new life == Similarly we have a death to sin, a death to our old life that was ruled by sin comes to an end. And then we have a new life we now live for God.

- Baptism represents our identification with Christ in His death, burial, and resurrection. Going beneath the water visibly pictures burial, and coming our pictures rising to new life. 

2. What does it mean to be dead to sin?
Paul says that our old self was crucified with Christ --> This means that sin no longer has the same authority over us that it once did. Before coming to Christ, we were under sin dominion. Through Christ, we have been freed from that dominion.

- Additionally when Paul says that Christ died to sin once for all, hes emphasizing the finality of Jesus victory over sins power. This sort of puts into context like the significance of the resurrection, he defated sin and hes stronger and reigns over sin, therefore his sacrifice is sufficient.

Justification = Being declared righteous before God through faith in Christ
Sanctification = The ongoing process of becoming more holy and living in a way that reflects our relationship with God.

3. Obedience from the heart
- Paul says believers have come to obey Gods teaching from thier hearts. This emphasizez that our obedience isnt something that should feel forced on us, or merely just compliance with the law. A Christian should obey God only to maintain a religious appearnce or gain approval of other people. Instead, obedience should flow from genuine faith and a changed heart.

4. Are we really free when were slaves to sin?
"If someone believes they're free because they can do whatever they want, but they're actually enslaved by sin, are they really free?"
- Someone who lives under sins control might believe they possess complete freedom because they dont feel obligated to obey God, but Paul challenges that understanding of freedom. Being able to follow every desire doesnt necessarily mean you`re free, espeically if those desires lead you straight to DEATH.

5. Wages of sin versus the gift of God
Romans 6:23 "For the wages of sin os death, but the gift of God is eternal life in Christ Jesus our Lord"
- Notice the use of words here: We use wages for sin, because wages are earned, but the "GIFT" of god is something we are given
