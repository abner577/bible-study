## Romans 9
Anguish = Refers to physical or spiritual suffering.
Reckoned = To take into account or consider as a hard fact.
Gods sovereignty = He posses supreme, absolute power and authoirt as Creator to rule over all creation without limitation

Chapter summary: Paul expresses his sorrow over Israel's rejection of Christ and explains that belonging to God's people is not simply a matter of physical ancestry. He emphasizes God's sovereignty in carrying out His promises, His freedom to show mercy, and how Gentiles have obtained righteousness through faith.

Paul addresses this question in Romans 9 --> If Israel was Gods chosen people, what have so many Israelities rejected Jesus as the Messiah? Does that mean Gods promise to Israel have failed?

1. Romans 9: 1-8 Not all who descen from Israel are Israel
- God's promises to Israel haven't failed because belonging to the people who inherit His promises has never been determined solely by physical descent.
- When Paul says that not all Abrahams descendants are Abrahams children, hes distinguishing between physical ancestry and belonging to the line of Gods promise.
- Paul uses Abrahams own family history to demonstrate that God`s covenant promises were never automatically inheritied by every biological descendant.

"through Issac that your offspring will be reckoned"?
- God specifically chose Issace as the son through who His covenant promises would continue. Paul is ilustrating Gods freedom to determine how His promises are fulfilled.

2. Romnans 9: 9-13
God promised Abraham and Sarah that they would have a son despite their old age. That son was Isaac. Isaac later married Rebecca, and they had twin sons: Esau and Jacob. Esau was born first, which normally gave him a privileged position as the firstborn.
However, before either child was born, God told Rebecca that the older would serve the younger. God chose Jacob rather than Esau as the one through whom the covenant line would continue.

Paul emphasizes that this decision was made before either brother had done anything good or bad. His point is that God's choice wasn't earned through their works. It originated in His own purpose and calling. This is why Paul says God's purpose in election would stand.

3. Romans 9:14-21
- Pauls main point is that mercy cant be demanded or earned. If God "owed" someone mercy because of their ancestry or obedience it would no longer be mercy in the same sense. Paul isnt arguring that God can just do whatever He wants without any concern for justice, but Paul is arguring that God remains rightoues while exercising sovereign authority over how His purposes unfold.

Why does God still blame us? --> Hypothetical objection
- Paul stated that God shows mercy to whom he chooses and hardens who He chooses. So someome might respond to that --> "If God ultimately determines what happens, how can He hold people responsible for their actions? Isn't everything happening according to His will anyway?"

- Paul objects with the analogy of the potter and caly to emphasize that human beings arent in a position to dictate how God must exercise His authority.

4. Romans 9:27-29 Isaiahs propheciy about Israel
- Paul quotes Isaiah to demonstrate that even within Israel, only a remnant would be preserved. 
Sodom and Gomorrah --> Sodom and Gomorrah were cities destroyed under Gods judgment in Genesis 19 --> When Isaiah says Israel would have become like Sodom and Gomorrah if God had not left descendants, he means that without God's mercy in preserving a remnant, Israel could have faced similarly complete destruction.

5. Romans 9:30-33
- Paul contrasts Gentiles who obtained righteousness throug faith with Israelities who pursued righteousness through the Law. The main problem was trying to establish righteousness through works rather than receiving it through faith.
