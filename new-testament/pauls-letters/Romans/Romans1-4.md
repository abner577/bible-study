## Romans 1
Chapter summary: Paul introduces himself to the Christians in Rome, expresses his desire to visit them, and emphasizes that the gospel brings salvation to everyone who believes. He then explains how humanity has rejected God despite the evidence of His existence, turning toward idolatry and sinful desires, demonstrating humanity's need for salvation.

1. Romans 1:8 - Thanking God through Jesus Christ
"First, I thank my God through Jesus Christ for all of you, because your faith is being reported all over the world." (Romans 1:8, NIV)
- This is just a nice reminder of the significance of Jesus Christ for all of us. He restores humanitys relationship with God.

2. God`s wrath against sinful humanity
Is paul talking about a specific group or humanity in general?
- Pauls immediate discussion particularly reflects the Gentile world, but Pauls larger argument is about all humanity.

3. What does "God have them over" mean?
- This means that God allowed people who persistently rejected Him to follow the sinful desires they had chosen, experiencing the consequences of that rejection.
- God reveals Himself to humanity, but people reject Him and choose to worship other things, God permits them to pursue the path they have chosen.

4. Humanitys sinful nature
- This is the larger argument that Paul is developing --> Hes first describing how deeply sinful humanity has become, and establishing the basis for why everyone needs **salvation**

## Romans 2
Chapter summary: Paul continues explaining humanity's sinful nature, but now turns his attention toward those who judge others while committing sins themselves. He emphasizes that God's judgment is impartial, that simply knowing or possessing God's Law does not make someone righteous, and that true obedience to God must come from within rather than merely through outward religious practices.

1. God`s kindness should lead us to repentance
- Paul is explaining that God's patience and kindness should never be interpreted as permission to continue sinning without consequences. What matter is the mentality/heart posture right:
If someone has the mentality along the lines of: "If God will forgive me anyway, why can't I continue sinning and just ask for forgiveness afterward?"

- True repentance isnt simply apologizing for something. It involves a genuine change of heart and a desire to turn away from sin. Our good works dont earn salvation, rather our actions should increasingly reflect the faith we profess.

3. God doesnt show favoritism: As you pointed out, Paul is dismantling the assumption that someone is automatically righteous because of their ancestry, religious background, or possession of God's Law.

4. Romans 2:29 -- Seeking praise from God, not people
- When Paul talks about a persons praise coming from God rather than people, hes emphasizing that what matters is Gods assesment of someones heart, not the outward religious identity that earns human recognition.

## Romans 3
forbearance = exercising self-restraint, and patiently enduring the faults or provocations of others without retaliating.

Chapter summary: Paul explains that God's faithfulness remains unchanged despite humanity's unfaithfulness and that everyone is under the power of sin. Since nobody can be declared righteous through obedience to the Law, Paul reveals that righteousness comes through faith in Jesus Christ, whose sacrifice makes it possible for sinners to be justified by God's grace.

- Everyone is sinful, nobody can earn righteousness through the Law, and salvation is available through faith in Jesus Christ

1. Does human unfaithfulness cancel Gods faithfulness?
- Paul raises the question: "If Gods chosen people were unfaithful to Him, does that mean God will abandon His promises to them?"
The answer is absolutely NOT --> Gods faithfulness doesnt depend on humanitys faithfullness. Even when humans are unfaithful, Gods remains true to His character and His promises

Then Paul makes a point: Something evil doesnt become morally good simply because God can bring something good out of it. Otherwise, someone could argue that we should deliberately commit sins so God would receive more glory.

2. Romans 3:9–20 — No one is righteous
- Nonbody can claim complete righteousness befoer God based on their obedience/works. Paul is emphasizing that no one possesses the perfect righteousness required to stand before God on the basis of their own merit.

4. Righteousness through faith
- So now Paul has established that nobody can make themselves righteous through works of the Law, so that begs the question --> How can we be made right with God??

Romans 3:22-24 explains that --> Righteousness is given through faith in Jesus Christ to all who believe, and that sinners are justified freely by Gods grace through Christ`s redemption.
Lets define a couple terms here:
    1. Justification = Being declared reighteous or put right with God
    2. Grace = Gods undeserved favor. Salvation is His gift, not something we can earn
    3. Atonement = Dealing with sin (paying the debt for it ) so that reconciliation with God becomes possible.

5. The relationship between faith, works, and obedience
- We don't perform good works to obtain salvation. Rather, genuine faith should lead us to desire to perform good works. Faith doesnt nullify good works or the Law, this is still important and good

## Romans 4
Chapter summary: Paul uses Abraham as an example to demonstrate that righteousness has always been credited through faith rather than earned through works or obedience to the Law. He explains that Abraham was declared righteous before being circumcised, showing that God's promise extends to both Jews and Gentiles who share Abraham's faith.

1. What does "credited to him as righteousness mean?"
So "We're kind of given the title of being righteous before God/with God, but we didn't do anything to really earn that."
- So this means we are credited with righteousnes, we are given this title of righteous but in reality we arent really that by ourselves, instead we were given that title by somebody. And that was through the death of Jesus Christ.

when Paul says Abraham's faith was credited to him as righteousness, he's explaining that God counted Abraham as righteous on the basis of faith, not because Abraham had earned that standing through perfect obedience.

2. Wages versus a gift
Paul distinguishes between 2 ways of receiving something:
- Wages = Something you receive because youve worked for it, youre OWED payment.
- Grace = Something freely given that you didnt earn.

- If righteousness came through our works, we could claim that God owed us salvation because we had fulfilled the requirements. But Paul's entire argument is that nobody can make such a claim. --> Because in order to technically do that we would need to be perfect, because thats the standard of God and Heaven, He IS Goodness and that embodies perfection.

3. Abraham was righteous before circumcision
- Abraham was counted righteous BEFORE he received circumcision. Therefore, circumcision couldnt have been what earned him righteousness.
- This connects to the idea of good works being evidence of genuine faith.
