## 1st Corinthians intro
- While Romans focuses heavily on the foundations of the Christian faith (sin, salvation, forgiveness, righthouess, faith and Gods grace) --> 1 Corinthians focuses more on how Christians hould live and conduct themselves, both individually and collectively as a church.
- Throughout this book, we see Paul addresses real problems within an established Christian community and explain how the teachings of Jesus should influence practically ever aspect of a believers life.

- Its main purposes was to correct problem within the church and instruct believers on Christian living, unity, and spiritual maturity.

## Historical and cultural context
- Corinth = A prosperous Roman city, it was an important cente rof commerce, trade, and culturual exchange.
- Corinth was home to people of diff ethnic background, including pagan worship (Paganism can refer to polytheism or the worship of more than one god).

- Understanding the culture of Corinth is important because many of the problems Paul addresses arose from Christians continuing to adopt the values and behaviors of the society around them. Although many of these people had accepted Christ, many were still struggling to separate their former way of life from their new identity as Christians.

## Who wrote it and what was Pauls relationship with the church?
If you remember from our reading of Acts 18, Paul had previously traveled to Corinth during his second missionary journey. There, he met Aquila and Priscilla, worked as a tentmaker and preached the gospel to both Jews and Gentiles. Paul remained in Corinth for a year and a half, teaching the word of God and establishing the church.
- Since Paul had personally established the church in Corinth, this meant he had an existing relationship with many of its believers and felt a personal responsibility for their spiritual development.

## Why was this book written?
1. First, Paul received reported from people associated with divisions within the church.
2. The Corinthians themselves had written to Paul asking for guidance on various matters. These included marriage, food sacrified to idlos and spiritual gifts.
    - Paul therefore writes both to correct existing problems and answer questions about how Christians should live.

## Main themes
1. Unity within the church
- Christians should be united in Christ rather than divided over human leaders or personal preferences.

2. Spiritual Maturity
- Believing in Christ should produce changes in one's character and behavior.

3. Human wisdom vs Gods wisdom
- God's truth is not dependent on human intelligence, philosophy or worldly status.

4. Sexual morality
- Believers should honor God with their bodies, which are temples of the Holy Spirit.

5. Christian freedom
- Being free in Christ does not mean that everything is beneficial or that we should disregard others.

6. Spiritual gifts
- Believers receive different gifts but belong to the same body and should serve one another.

7. Love
- Love should be the foundation of everything Christians do, including the exercise of spiritual gifts.

8. Order within the church
- Worship and church gatherings should glorify God and build up the community.
