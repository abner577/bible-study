## Question
Like who are the main groups of people that we deal with in the bible, jews? israelis? romans? etc. Explain these

*Israelite and Israeli are NOT interchangeable they are different*
- A israelite is someone belonging to ancient Israel, especially someone descended from Jacob, whose name God changed to Israel. An israeli today means a citizen of the modern State of Israel. In the bible, we will be dealing with Israelites not israelis.

### 1. Israelites (israelitas)
- Probably the most important group to understand in the Old Testament.

- The story begins with Abraham. God makes a covenant (a solemn, binding promise or agreement between God and humanity) with Abraham and promises that his descendants will become a great nation. The family line goes:
Abraham --> Issac --> Jacob --> Jacob has 12 sons, and God changes Jacobs name to Israel. 

- Therefore jacobs descendants become known as the children of Israel or Israelites --> The 12 sons of Jacob eventually become the 12 tribes of Israel. 
- So originally being an Israelite was primarily about family ancestry and belonging to this covenant people. It wasnt simply about "I believe in the israelite religion".
- They were the people desencded from Jacob/Israel and organized into a nation.

- Their religion, was also deeply connected to their identity because God had established a covenant with them. They worshiped the God of Israel and were given the Law through Moses (Moses writing the first 5 books of the old Testaement (Law category))

---

### 2. Hebrews
- The israelites living in Egypt are frequently describes as Hebrews. Hebrew is an older cultural description connected with the people who would become israel. 

A simple way to thinking about it is:
- Hebrews is an early term for Abrahams people and their descendants
- Israelites becomes the major term once Jacob/Israels descendant become the nation of Israel
- Jews become especially common later in Israels history and throughout the New Testament

### 3. Jews
- As seen above, by the time we reach the New Testament, "Jew" is the main term we are going to encounter.
- Jesus was a Jew, Mary was Jewish, Joseph was Jewish, Peter was Jewish, Paul was Jewish, The Twelve Apostles were Jewish.

- Christianity itself begins within Judaism, not as something originally coming from Romans or Europeans.

- The word Jew comes from Judah --> One of Israels 12 tribes/sons: One of those tribes was Judah. Much later the kingdom of Israel splits into 2 kingdoms:
1. Northern Kingdom --> Israel
2. Southern Kingdom --> Judah

- Jerusalem was located in the southern Kingdom of Judah, eventually the northern kingdom was conquered by the Assyrians --> Later, Judah was conquered by the Babylonians, Jerusalem was destroyed, and many people were taken into exile.
- The surviving people associated with Judah become known increasingly as Jews --> Over time "Jew" became much broader it didnt just mean you were from the tribe of Judah instead it came to refer to the people of Israel as an ethnic and religious community.

- So by Jesus` time, Jewish identity involved both ancestry/peoplehood and religion. People could also convert to Judaism, so ancestry wasnt the only possible way someone could become Jewish.

### 4. Jesus was Jewish
Jesus was born into a Jewish family and most of the people Jesus interacts with during the Gospels are Jewish. Christianity hadnt yet developed into the large distinct worldwide religion we think of today. Jesus earliest followers believed that he was the promised Jewish Messiah.


### 5. Gentiles
- A Gentile is anyone who isnt Jewish. This becomes extremely important in the New Testamenet because one of the huge questions of the early Church becomes: "Can Gentiles become followers of Jesus without first becoming Jews (i.e. following Jewish traditions like circumcision") --> The answer the early Church eventually gives is yes.


### 6. Romans
- Romans are something completely differnet. Roman is primarily a political/national identity, during Jesus lifetime, the Roman Empire controlled Judea, where Jesus lived. So we have Jewish people living under Roman politicl rule. 

### 7. Greeks
- By New Testament times, Greek culture had spread throughout much of the Mediterranean beacuse of the earlier conquests of Alexander the Great. Even though Rome controlled the region politically, Greek language and culture remained extremely influential.
- This is why the New Testament itself was written primarily in Greek. But someimtes New Testaement writers use "Greek" almost like "Gentiles".

### 8. Samaritans
- They are closely related to the Israelites, but Jews and Samaritans had a long history of religious and ethnic conflict. After the northern Kingdom of Israel was conquered by Assyria, the region went through major population and religious changes.
- The Samaritans eventually developed their own community centered around worship of the God of Israel, but they disagreed with Jews about several major things, including where God should properly be worshiped.
- By Jesus time, there was considerable hostility between many Jews and Samartians. Thats why stories such as the Good Samaritan are more shocking than they initially sound to modern readers. 

### 9. Pharisees
- The Pharisees were Jews, they were essentially a religious movement within Judaism. Jesus frequently critcizes certain Pharisees, especially for hypocrisy, pride, and focusing on outward religious behavior while neglecting deeper matters such as mercy and justice. 
- So the Pharisees were one Jewish religious group among several.

### 10. Sadducees
- The Sadducees were another Jewish group --> Especially associated with:
- The Temple, priests, welath/aristocratic families, and religious leadership.

- They disagreed with the Pharisees on several theological questions, for example:
- The Pharisees beleived in the resurrection of the dead, while the Sadducees did not. 

### 11. Scribes / Teachers of the Law
- These people are experts in the Jewish Scripture and Law they are somewhat like religous/legal scholars.

### 12. Priests and Levites (Both Testaments)
- One of the 12 tribes of Israel was Levi. The tribe of Levi was set apart for religious services, from within Levi came the priesthood.

- **Levites** --> Members of the tribe of Levi associated with religious service.
- **Priests** --> Particular Levites descended through Aaron who performed priestly duties.

- By Jesus time the High Priest and leading priests were extremely powerful figures in Jerusalem.

### 13. Canaanites (Old Testament)
- Canaan is the land God promises to Abrahams descendants. When the Israelites come out of Egypt under Moses and later enter the land under Joshua, Canaan is already inhabited by different peoples collectively referred to as Canaanites.
- A recurring Old Testament problem is Israel adopting the religious practices of surronding Canaanite people instead of remaining faithful to Yahweh.

### 14. Philistines
- The Philistines were one of Israel`s major enemies during the period before and during the early monarchy. Famous stories involve them such as: David vs Goliath as Goliath was a Philistine.

### 15. Egyptians
- Egypt appears repeatedly throughout the bible, the israelities eventually lived in Egypt and were enslaved there. Egypt later continues appearing as a neighboring political power.

### 16. Assyrians
- Israel eventually splits into the Northern Kingdom Israel and the Southern Kingdom being Judah.
- Assyria conquers the northern kingdom, afterward the northern kingdom essentially disapperas as an independent kingdom.

### 17. Babylonians
- Babylon conquers Judah and destroys Jerusalem and the Temple. Many Jews are taken into exile in Babylon --> This is the famous Babylonian Exile (that was mentioned when going through Matthews geneaology).
- This event is very important because it becomes one of the biggest turning points in the entire Old Testament we have:
1. Israel before exile 
& 
2. Israel after Exile

### 18. Persians
- Eventually the Babylonians Empire is conquered by the Persian Empire. The Persian king Cyrus allows the Jewish exiles to return to Jerusalem, this is when you get books such as: Ezra and Nehemiah --> and the rebuilding of Jerusalem and the Temple.
